02

Perspective

How I think about data and AI after two decades of making healthcare data usable.

AI works at three levels

Most organizations start with the first level. The real advantage comes from working on all three at once.

Inside the productGenAI experiences that help users get from data to decisions faster.
Inside the data platformPatient risk factors, provider performance metrics, enrichment and cohort intelligence.
Inside the organizationAI-assisted development, testing, documentation and observability, and later agentic engineering workflows.

LLMs can infer meaning. Architecture has to provide authoritative meaning.

A model can guess that rx_id is a prescription ID. It can't know your definition of an active prescription, which source wins when two systems disagree, or whether two provider IDs are the same person. Those are enterprise semantics, not language problems. The objective isn't the knowledge graph. It's trusted meaning.

When machines consume data, metadata becomes runtime

Analysts tolerate ambiguity, and they know which table to trust. Agents can't. So the platform has to tell them what entities exist, how they relate, where data came from, what quality applies and what actions are allowed. Semantics move from documentation into the runtime.

Controlled workflows first, autonomy where it's needed

I don't introduce a multi-agent framework unless the use case requires it. For deterministic business processes I prefer controlled workflows, and I give the model more autonomy only where the decision path can't be set in advance.

When a client says "we want AI"

  1. Understand the business objectives
  2. Identify the decisions to improve
  3. Assess data readiness
  4. Prioritize high-value use cases
  5. Build an MVP
  6. Measure business impact
  7. Scale what works

04

Expertise

Healthcare data is generated for billing, dispensing and care, not for analysis. Knowing how it's generated is what makes it trustworthy.

Three views of the data

Patient

Longitudinal journeys, cohort eligibility, initiation, switching, adherence and persistence, dosing, concomitant therapy.

Where do patients start, switch or drop off?

Payer & access

Payer and plan mapping, coverage mix, paid, rejected and reversed claims, out-of-pocket costs, remittance.

Where do access and affordability barriers appear?

Provider

HCP profiles and specialty, prescribing patterns, segmentation, referral networks, provider and patient relationships.

Which providers matter, and how do they differ?

The foundation under every view

Entity resolution

Matching and mastering patients, payers, providers and other industry identifiers across sources, so one person or organization is one record.

Cleansing & standardization

Normalizing codes, statuses and formats from many vendors into consistent, reusable data.

Data governance

Lineage, quality controls, access policies and clear ownership, so every number can be traced and trusted.

HIPAA & de-identification

Privacy built into the platform: patient de-identification, PII/PHI detection and quarantine, and HIPAA-compliant data handling.

Each one powers two kinds of work

Commercial analytics

Market share, source of business, new-to-brand, targeting, hospital sales and spillover, brand lifecycle analytics.

Where is brand growth coming from?

Real-world evidence

Outcomes research, treatment patterns, cohort studies and comparative analyses built on the same longitudinal data.

What happens to patients in real-world care?
